This thesis evaluates \gls{llm}-driven decision-making for heterogeneous multi-robot collaboration in a task-assignment warehouse setting with battery constraints.
The implemented control loop grounds decisions to discrete environment action identifiers, queries an \gls{llm} using centralized or per-agent shared-context prompts, and enforces feasibility through action-mask validation.

The reported results cover the shelf-delivery, call-aware, and battery-aware objective families. The shelf-delivery objective provides the data for planning-architecture and prompt-format comparisons. The call-aware and battery-aware objectives provide the data for the objective-sensitivity analysis in the shared-context natural-language setting.

Within the available data, natural-language prompting produced higher delivery throughput and higher call-normalised delivery efficiency than \gls{json} prompting across the reported scenario and model groupings. Shared-context planning produced higher mean deliveries than centralized planning in the scenario-level comparison, while centralized planning used fewer \gls{llm} calls. The objective-sensitivity analysis showed mixed adaptation: the call-aware objective did not consistently reduce \gls{llm} usage, and battery-aware productivity varied by model.
